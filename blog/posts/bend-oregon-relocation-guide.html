Relocating to Bend, Oregon: An Honest Local's Guide

By Christian Lisignoli  ·  September 10, 2026  ·  7 min read

I grew up in Central Oregon. I've watched Bend go from a quiet timber town to one of the fastest-growing cities in the country — and I've helped dozens of people relocate here over the years. Some love it from day one. Others arrive expecting one thing and find something different.

This guide is the honest version. Not a tourism pitch — a real look at what life in Bend is actually like, so you can make a clear-eyed decision about whether it's the right move for you.

Why People Move to Bend

The draw is real. Bend sits at 3,600 feet on the eastern slope of the Cascades — high desert country, surrounded by ponderosa pines, volcanic landscape, and some of the best outdoor recreation in the Pacific Northwest. The city gets over 300 days of sunshine a year and only about 12 inches of rain. That's less than Los Angeles.

People come here for the lifestyle. Skiing at Mt. Bachelor in the morning, mountain biking Phil's Trail in the afternoon, craft beer downtown at night. The Deschutes River runs right through town. Smith Rock State Park is 30 minutes north. The Cascades are in your backyard.

But beyond the outdoor access, what keeps people here is the community. Bend has a small-city feel with a lot of depth to it — strong local food scene, an active arts community, people who are genuinely invested in where they live. It's the kind of place where you run into people you know at the farmers market.

What Bend Gets Right

The Outdoor Access Is Unmatched

300+ miles of mountain bike trails, world-class skiing at Mt. Bachelor, rock climbing at Smith Rock, river floating on the Deschutes, hiking to Tumalo Falls, kayaking the Cascade Lakes. You're not driving hours to get to the good stuff — it's all within 30 minutes. If outdoor recreation is part of how you live, Bend delivers on that promise.

The Weather

The sunshine is one of Bend's most underrated features. The high desert climate means dry, clear summers and cold but mostly sunny winters. You get real seasons without the relentless grey that defines the Willamette Valley. Summers rarely top the mid-80s. It doesn't feel like a Pacific Northwest city — it feels more like Colorado.

No Sales Tax

Oregon has no state sales tax. Every purchase — groceries, furniture, a car — is what the sticker says. Over the course of a year, especially for families, this adds up meaningfully. It softens the blow of the higher housing costs a bit.

A Real Downtown

Downtown Bend is walkable, local, and genuinely good. More than 30 craft breweries, strong restaurant options, independent shops, live music venues, and the Old Mill District along the river. It punches well above its size for a city of around 110,000 people.

Strong Schools

The Bend-La Pine School District is well-regarded, particularly on the west side. Summit High School consistently ranks among the top public high schools in Oregon. If you're relocating with kids, the school quality is a genuine asset.

What to Know Before You Move

The Cost of Living Is Real

This is the one that trips people up most. Bend's overall cost of living runs roughly 20–30% above the national average, and housing is the primary driver. The median home price in 2026 is approximately $670,000–$725,000. Entry-level homes — move-in ready, Bend proper — generally start in the $500s. A 2-bedroom apartment runs $1,600–$2,300 a month depending on the neighborhood.

If you're relocating from a high-cost California or Seattle market, Bend may look affordable by comparison. If you're coming from anywhere in the middle of the country, it's a real adjustment. Run your numbers before you move, not after.

The Job Market Is Narrower Than You'd Expect

Bend's economy is anchored by healthcare (St. Charles Health System is the largest employer), tourism, the outdoor recreation industry, retail, and construction. Tech presence is growing but still limited. Remote workers have flooded into Bend over the past several years — if you're location-independent, that makes it easy. If you need to find local employment in a specialized field, research the market carefully before committing.

Wildfire Smoke in Summer

The summers are beautiful, but smoke season is real. Central Oregon can see extended stretches of poor air quality from regional wildfires, sometimes for several weeks. It's a genuine quality-of-life consideration, especially for anyone with respiratory issues. Most locals have air purifiers and learn to plan around it.

Winters at Elevation

Bend gets an average of 33 inches of snow per year. The town itself handles it well, but you'll want all-season tires at a minimum and ideally four-wheel drive if you're heading to the mountains regularly. Roads can ice up, and mountain passes require chains or AWD. It's manageable, but it's not a mild coast city.

Traffic Has Gotten Worse

Bend's infrastructure hasn't fully kept pace with its growth. A few intersections — particularly on the south end of town and the Third Street corridor — see real congestion during peak hours. It's nothing like a major metro, but if you're expecting a sleepy small town, adjust expectations. The city is actively working on it, but road expansion takes time.

Limited Public Transit

Bend is a car city. The public bus system (Cascades East Transit) exists but is limited in hours and coverage. If you're used to getting around without a vehicle, plan accordingly. Most residents drive everywhere.

Neighborhoods: Where to Look

Bend divides roughly into west side and east side, with meaningfully different character and price points on each.

Westside

Northwest Crossing (NWX)

Bend's most recognizable master-planned community. Walkable, with its own shops, restaurants, parks, and a Saturday farmers market. Strong schools nearby. Consistently one of the most in-demand neighborhoods for families. Homes typically run $750K–$1.1M.

Westside

Awbrey Butte

Elevated neighborhood with panoramic Cascade views. Larger lots, established feel, luxury price points. Homes start around $1.1M and climb well above that for the best view properties. The market has softened slightly at this tier — buyers have more negotiating room than a few years ago.

Westside — Resort

Tetherow & Broken Top

Gated and resort-style communities in southwest Bend. Golf, privacy, ponderosa canopy. Tetherow runs $1.5M and up; Broken Top is Bend's most established luxury enclave. Worth the premium if you'll actually use the amenities — less clear if you won't.

Central / Eastside

Old Bend & Orchard District

Established central neighborhoods close to downtown. Older housing stock, more character, generally more accessible price points ($550K–$720K). The Orchard District in particular is going through a quiet revitalization — good area to watch for value.

Eastside

Mountain View & Larkspur

The east side offers a Bend address at meaningfully lower prices — typically $550K–$700K. Less trail access than the west side, more practical and residential. Good option for first-time buyers or anyone who needs to stretch the budget without leaving city limits.

Note: If your budget is under $500K, look at Redmond (17 miles north, median around $445K). It's a real town with its own character — not just a Bend suburb — and it's where a lot of smart buyers are landing right now.

The Housing Market in 2026

If you're planning to buy, here's where things stand. The median home price in Bend sits around $670,000–$725,000 heading into fall 2026. Inventory has improved from the pandemic lows, but move-in-ready homes under $550,000 within city limits are rare — under 15% of active listings.

The market is more balanced than it was in 2021–2022, which means you have more time to make decisions and more room to negotiate, especially in the upper price tiers. That said, well-priced homes in the $600K–$800K range on the west side still move quickly when they show up.

A few things worth knowing if you're buying:

  • West side commands a meaningful premium over east side — for good reason, but make sure you're paying for something you'll actually use
  • New construction is happening primarily in the outer areas and in Redmond — good option if you want modern finishes without a major renovation
  • Duplexes and properties with ADUs are worth a close look — rental income can meaningfully offset your costs in a tight rental market
  • Property taxes here are relatively low compared to most states — Bend runs around 0.60% of assessed value annually

Is Bend Right for You?

Good Fit If...

  • Outdoor access is a priority
  • You're remote or location-flexible
  • You value community over nightlife
  • You can afford the housing market
  • You want four real seasons
  • You're drawn to a smaller, intentional city

Think Twice If...

  • You need specialized local employment
  • Budget caps below $500K for housing
  • You rely on public transportation
  • Summer smoke is a health concern
  • You want major-city energy and diversity
  • You're not drawn to outdoor living

Bend rewards people who move here for the right reasons. The ones who struggle are usually the ones who moved for the Instagram version of the city without thinking through the cost of living or the job market. Do your homework, come visit in winter (not just summer), and make sure the lifestyle matches how you actually live — not how you want to live.
